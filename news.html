13 October 2026 · Version 1.8

A lift can now ask for a different set each time.

Most gym apps hold your sets and give them back to you. REGOL reads them: your reps, and how many you had left in the tank, and then proposes the next weight with the reasoning written underneath. Version 1.8 is the biggest update since launch, and it goes after the one thing that was still too blunt — the idea that every set of a lift should ask for the same thing.

Set by set

Turn Set by set on in a lift's prescription and each set gets its own rep range and its own target reps in reserve: 8 to 10 with two left, then two more at one, then a last set with nothing in the tank. The engine then judges every set against the rung it was actually asked for.

That sounds like a detail. It is the difference between being read correctly and not. Finish a ramp exactly as written and your effort drops across the session by design — and against a single flat target that looks like fatigue, so the weight would be held. Read set by set, the same session is dead on target and earns the step. If you train with top sets and back-offs, or anything that gets harder as it goes, this is the release that understands it.

Why these weights

Today's card now opens the engine's reasoning for every lift in the session at once, in the same words you will see on the live screen when you get there. And the label beside each weight says what was actually decided: a weight that holds says holds. Red is kept for a weight that genuinely comes down.

Every lift's chart, full size

A lift's page opens its chart on its own screen — estimated one-rep max, top set or volume, with every session listed underneath and a point you can tap to jump to the day behind it. From there it shares as a card of its own.

Cards worth posting

The share cards were rebuilt. A workout card lists every lift rather than the first four. A progress card shows every lift in the programme. The all-time card draws the running total that produced the number, and a record card draws the run-up the record sits on the end of. All of them can be set to kilograms, pounds, or exactly as you logged it — because the person you send it to was not there.

Plans is two tabs

Programs and Templates are separate now, so the workout you are about to edit is not three screens below the programme card. Your training block also shows as a bar for each week with the deload marked at the end, so where you are in it is something you see rather than something you read.

And about a hundred and twenty smaller things

Every screen in the app was read line by line before this release. Names that were cut short with room to spare, numbers clipped by columns too narrow to hold them, counts that read "1 workouts", a record screen that lost its words at large text sizes: all fixed. None of it is the sort of thing anyone writes a headline about, and all of it is the sort of thing you notice every session.

Nothing about your training moved

The same engine reads the same file on your phone. No weight you have logged changed because of this update, and nothing was uploaded anywhere — REGOL still has no accounts, no analytics and no ads, and your training never leaves your device.
