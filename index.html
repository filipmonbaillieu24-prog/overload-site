The problem

Most gym apps are notebooks.

They remember what you did. Then they leave you standing at the rack doing the one thing an app should do for you: working out what to lift next. So you guess. Too light, and the week is wasted. Too heavy, and you grind to a stop.

The one extra tap

How many more could you have done?

That number is called reps in reserve. Zero means you hit failure. Two means you had two more in you. You tap it once after every set, and it is the difference between an app that stores numbers and one that understands them.

Skip it and the set is recorded at the effort you planned, so nothing breaks. The honest tap is what earns you an accurate next weight.

The answer

Then REGOL reads it, and writes down the next weight.

Before you start, every lift in the workout already has its weight on the card. Tap Why these weights and each one explains itself in plain words. The same sentence waits for you on the live screen, so there is never a number without a reason.

The step size

It never jumps further than your reps can carry.

Your best set says roughly what you could lift once. From that, REGOL checks whether the next step up would still let you reach the bottom of your rep range. On a heavy barbell, 2.5 kg is nothing. On a 10 kg dumbbell it is a quarter of the weight, so instead of sending you up, it asks for one more rep.

Clear the top of your rep range on every set and the weight goes up regardless. Past the top there is no rep left to add.

Between sets

Rest you can read from across the room.

Log a set and the rest timer starts on its own and fills the screen. Put the phone down on the bench and you can still read it. It rings and vibrates when the rest ends, even on silent, and the next set is already filled in when you pick the phone back up.

After the last set

Every new best gets a moment.

Beat a record and the screen takes over to say so, with a buzz you will feel through the bench. It is the only celebration in the app, so it means something. Then the summary lists each best, the set that beat it, and next time’s weight for every lift.

One line at the top reads the session for you: your heaviest in four months, a lift that moved for the first time since August. Observation, never praise.

Weeks later

Proof the program is working.

Every lift’s first working weight beside its latest, since the day you started the program. Each lift also has its own page: estimated one-rep max over time, your heaviest sets for 1, 3, 5, 8 and 10 reps, and every session you have logged.

If a lift goes four sessions without a new best, REGOL says so and offers a lighter week, a wider rep range, or a swap.

Under the hood

Built for how people actually train.

A different set each timeA lift can ask for 8 to 10 with two left, then one left, then nothing. Each set is judged against what it asked for, so a top set and back-offs read correctly.
Training blocksHarder week by week, then a lighter one. The week turns only when every workout in it is done, so it moves with your training, not the calendar.
First time on a liftNo history, no guessing. A few test sets find your working weight, and the next one is aimed from your answer.
Warm-ups that know the planThey ramp to the day’s weight, and shrink when the muscle is already warm from an earlier lift.
Every kind of setSupersets, drop sets, rest-pause, AMRAP, timed holds. Warm-ups and drops are logged but never move the weight. A set to failure counts double.
Share with friendsHand a friend a template or a whole program as a code their camera reads, or as a file. Everything travels inside the code, so it works with no server and no signal.

The engine is tested against 33,705 recorded training situations before every release, so a weight it proposed last month is the weight it proposes today.

Make it yours

Five accents. Three themes. Or a colour of your own.

Dark, Light, or true Black for OLED screens and night sessions. Pick a preset or dial in your own hue. Every colour is held to at least 7:1 contrast against the black ink on top of it, so a button is readable at any brightness. A program can carry its own colour too, worn only while you train it.

Your data

Your training never leaves your phone.

There is no account to make and no server holding your history. Everything lives in one file on your phone, which is why the app works in a basement gym with no signal. Export it as JSON or CSV whenever you like, or have it backed up to a folder you choose after every workout.

Questions

Asked first.

Something else? Support has the rest.

Where do I get it?

On Google Play, for Android 8.0 and up. Want a look first? Run the whole app in your browser.

Does it work offline?

Yes, completely. The only thing that ever needs a connection is checking your subscription with Google Play, and the last answer is remembered.

Do I have to log reps in reserve?

It is one tap per set, and it is what makes the proposals good. If you skip it, the set is recorded at the effort you planned, so the engine reads the workout as on plan.

Can I bring my history from another app?

Yes. Strong and Hevy CSV exports import directly, and REGOL matches them to your lifts. How to import.

Is there a catch to it being free?

No. Everything is free for everyone until 31 December 2026, with no payment method asked for. From 2027 the app falls back to the free plan unless you subscribe, and nothing you made is deleted either way.

What happens to my data if I stop paying?

Nothing is deleted or hidden. You keep training on the free plan, and everything stays exportable. Templates and programs beyond the free limit wait, greyed out, until you subscribe again.

iPhone?

Not yet. REGOL is Android only. iOS is on the roadmap, after the Android app has stopped changing shape.
