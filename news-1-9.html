News/Version 1.9·5 November 2026

Six questions in your browser, and a week lands on your phone.

The biggest release so far, in ten chapters. Nothing you have logged moves because of it: the engine’s 33,705 recorded answers are byte-identical to 1.8, so every weight it proposes and every sentence it says are the ones you already know.

The builder

Six questions, and a week comes back.

This site asks six things — days a week, what you can train with, how long you have been lifting, how long a session can run, which muscle groups to push or leave alone, and whether it runs as a block — and writes the whole rotation.

It arrives as a code. Point the phone at the one on screen, or open the link. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account, because the program travels inside the code itself — which is also why it works with the network off. Every number is yours to change before you send it, and after it lands.

The page says what a week of it asks of each muscle before you commit to it.
A card that is a code

Hand somebody the training, not a picture of it.

Sharing a finished workout can now send the session itself. The card carries a code, and whoever scans it gets that workout — or the whole rotation it belongs to — as workouts in their own Plans.

They keep their own weights. A lift they already train keeps their numbers, and a new one starts light and lets the engine find the weight from there, because a load that suits you is not a load that suits them.

There is no server to fetch it from. The training is inside the code, so it reads with the network off.
Starting from nothing

A new install opens on three ways in, not a list of chores.

It used to say add lifts, then build a template, in that order, which made the Lifts tab a gate in front of everything else. Now: make a template, start an empty workout and add the lifts as you go, or use the builder on this site. There is nothing to collect first, because the picker searches the bundled library by name from either of the first two.

A short welcome, and a tour that points at the real controls rather than describing them. Plans shows its Programs and Templates tabs from the start, instead of only once there is something in them. And the new-lift form asks the two questions that matter, where nine rows used to ask everything at once.

Nothing to collect first. Search the library by name from inside the workout you already started.
Stats

It opens with the three numbers it is about.

All three were already on the page and none was readable without work: the tonnage was a numeral you had to scroll to, the hard-set total was a sum of fifteen rows nobody adds up, and the session count was on another tab. Each tile carries its comparison, because the question under all three is more or less than last week — and a first week says so rather than comparing itself against nothing.

The hard-set bar was scaled to the top of the target, so a muscle at the target and one at twice it filled the bar identically. The target is a band on the track now, and past the top is warn-coloured rather than accent: over is not a better version of on target.

The weekly chart marks the weeks you logged nothing, so a fortnight off and a quiet week stop looking the same, and draws a dashed average across the weeks that have something in them. Empty weeks are left out of that average: a fortnight off is not evidence about how much you lift.

The Log tab

Your week, as a card and a row of bars.

The log opens on the week you are in rather than a list that starts at the top and keeps going, and its filters fit a 360dp phone instead of running off the right edge of one.

The calendar says what the day you tapped actually was, and weeks on goal moved there too, beside the streak it belongs next to. Biggest movers get a sparkline: two endpoints cannot tell a steady climb from a jump in week two and ten weeks of nothing after it, and the second of those is the one worth knowing about.

A session card says the lifts, and nothing after their names.
You

Seven pages instead of one long screen.

Every setting in the app used to live on one scroll, each with its explanation printed underneath it. You is a hub of seven pages now, and the explanations moved behind an (i) beside the thing they explain — so a row is a row, and the paragraph is there when you want it.

The builders went the same way. Four sections of a template became four rows, and the numbers in them became tiles you can read without opening anything. One control, one name, too: the exercise board is called the exercise board everywhere it appears.

A row is a row. The (i) is there when you want the paragraph.
On your lock screen

The workout notice is a card now, and it says why.

It was a form. It is a card: the lift you are on, where you are in it, and buttons you can read without unlocking the phone.

Three things it never said. The engine’s reason for today’s weight — the layout had carried an empty slot for it since the day it was written. A record, on the resting card, which is the state you are in a second after the set that beat your best. And on Android 16, a short position in the status-bar chip.

Its greys are the app’s greys. The notification and the widget each carried a hand-written near-black copied from the share cards, which are a green near-black on purpose because a card leaves the app and cannot follow your theme. It is the kind of difference nobody can name and everybody can see.

A view nobody fills is a hole with a label on it.
From January 2027

Three ways to pay, and a free plan that keeps your proof.

By the month, by the year, or bought outright. Google Play moves you between monthly and yearly itself, so nothing has to be cancelled first. Buying outright is owned rather than active: nothing renews, there is nothing to cancel, and it survives a new phone.

Free is three templates, one program, and the last eight weeks of anything drawn against time. Nothing is deleted and nothing is hidden: a template outside the plan still shows its lifts and its history, it just cannot be started. Your records stay all-time for everybody, and so does progress since you started — the one screen that proves the engine works belongs on the free side of the line.

All of it is free for everyone through 31 December 2026, and anyone with a session logged before then keeps the full app until 1 January 2028. That is read from your own training log rather than from a flag something had to set at the right moment, so it holds on a new phone.

Also in 1.9

Things you can see, and things you could not.

Three groups of options were marked by a fill measuring 1.01:1 against the cell beside them on the light theme, and five more told a screen reader they were radio buttons without ever saying which one was on. The weekly chart draws each past week as a bar whose height is the number, at 1.22:1 against the card under it. All three were measured on a device, and all three are fixed.

A rep range gets a row per end. The muscle groups of a lift you already have can be changed. Template cards carry a time estimate. An import from another app works out your routines instead of flooding Lifts with stall cards. And the ten-second warning before a rest ends is gone.

Fixed: saving a program no longer activates one you only opened to look at, reordering no longer moves you off the lift you were on, swap stops offering you the lift you are swapping, the Lift page stops proposing week one’s weight in week five, and Plans stops calling a program inactive while Today counts its weeks.

Same engine, same file on your phone. 976 tests pass, none skipped.
