The principle

What is progressive overload?

Asking your body for slightly more over time than it is used to: more weight, more reps, more quality sets. The principle is simple; the hard part is knowing when.

The short answer

Progressive overload means asking your body for slightly more over time than it is used to. More weight is the obvious way. More reps at the same weight, more quality sets, or the same work with better control all count too.

The word progressive is doing the work. Not more every session, and not more at any price - more often enough, and read from what you actually did.

The ways to progress

More weight. The clearest one, and the one that runs out fastest.

More reps. Keep the weight and add a rep. Over a rep range this is where most progress actually happens, and it is why rep ranges exist: eight to twelve gives you four sessions of progress before the weight has to move.

More sets. Useful, with a ceiling. There is a point past which extra sets cost recovery without buying anything.

Better sets. Fuller range of motion, less help from the rest of you. Harder to measure, which is why it is usually left out of apps, including this one.

Why it stalls

Every scheme works until it does not. Adding a fixed amount every week works for a beginner because almost anything works for a beginner, and it breaks the moment your recovery stops keeping up with the arithmetic.

A stall is usually not a failure of effort. It is a sign that the step was too big, the range was too narrow, or the lift needs a lighter week before it can go up again.

Going backwards on purpose

A deload is a planned lighter session or week. It feels like losing ground and it is the opposite: it is how you arrive at the next heavy week able to lift. Blocks that get harder week by week and end in a lighter one are built on exactly this.

How REGOL does it

You log the weight, the reps, and how many reps you had left. From your second session on, every lift carries a proposed weight worked out from what you actually lifted, and a sentence saying why.

It prefers reps before weight, sizes each step from your own sets rather than a fixed increment, offers a lighter session when you come in under target twice, and notices when a lift has gone four workouts without a new best - then offers a deload, a wider rep range, or a swap.

Nothing is automatic in the sense of being out of your hands. Every number can be overruled with one tap, and every proposal shows its working so you can disagree with a reason rather than a feeling.
