Two or three days a week

Full-body training

Every session trains everything. It is the most forgiving way to lift when your week is unpredictable, and the easiest to start with. Here is a complete week to take with you.

ON THIS PAGE The short answerThe weekWho it suitsMaking it three daysHow you progressTake it with you

The short answer

A full-body session trains your legs, your pushing and your pulling in the same workout. Nothing waits a week for its turn, which is why missing a session costs you less here than in any split.

The cost is that each session has to cover a lot, so no single muscle gets many sets in one go. That is fine, and it is the reason full-body programmes lean on compound lifts.

The week

A real week out of REGOL's program builder: 2 workouts, 40 working sets. The two days are different enough to be worth doing both - different main lifts, different angles - rather than the same session twice.

The number beside each lift is its sets, and the range after it is the reps to aim for.

Who it suits

It suits you if you are starting out, coming back after a break, or if your week genuinely cannot promise three or four fixed evenings. Two full-body sessions you actually do beat four you keep missing.

It suits you less once you want more volume per muscle than a shared session can carry. That is usually the moment to move to push, pull, legs or upper/lower.

Making it three days

Three full-body sessions a week is the classic version, and the builder will make one: answer three days instead of two, or take this week and add a third workout in the app. A third day mostly adds sets rather than new lifts, so expect it to look like a cousin of these two rather than something new.

How you progress on it

A split decides what you train on which day. It does not decide whether you get stronger - that is what you do with the weight week to week.

The short version: finish the top of your rep range on every working set, or finish with more left in reserve than you planned, and the weight goes up. Neither, and it stays while you chase reps. When to add weight goes through both signals, and what RIR means covers the second one.
