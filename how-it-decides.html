The rules, in full

How REGOL decides your next weight

After every session REGOL works out what you should lift next time, one lift at a time, and tells you in a sentence why. This page is the whole rulebook behind those sentences.

What it reads

Three numbers per set: the weight, the reps you got, and how many reps you had left when you stopped — reps in reserve.

The third is the one most logbooks never ask for, and it is the one that makes a decision possible. Eight reps at 80 kg with four left in the tank and eight reps at 80 kg with nothing left are the same line in a spreadsheet and two completely different sessions.

Everything below is arithmetic on your own log, on your own phone. There is no account, nothing is uploaded, and there is no model guessing at you.

When the weight goes up

The weight rises when the session says the current one no longer asks enough of you. That is one of three things:

  • You did more reps than the top of your range, on every set.
  • You reached the top of your range on every set and it felt as hard as you planned it to feel.
  • You finished with more reps left in the tank than you aim for, and adding reps instead would take you past the top of your range.

The sentence names the decision first and the reason second, always in that order:

Up 2.5 kg to 82.5 kg. You got 8 reps on every set, the most you aim for, and it felt as hard as planned. That is everything this weight asks of you.

A step is a rung on that lift’s own ladder, not a fixed number of kilos. On a plain barbell those are the same thing. On a stack with a 2.3 kg add-on magnet they are not, and REGOL takes the gentle rung when there is a gentle rung to take.

When it stays the same

Holding is the most common answer, and it is a decision rather than the absence of one. It comes with its own reason.

If you had reps left over but there is still room to add reps at this weight, REGOL asks for the reps first:

Same weight. You had about 3 reps left in the tank, one more than the 2 you aim for, so get more reps at this weight first. Reach 8 reps on every set and it goes up.

If the session went exactly as planned, the answer is a single rep: Try for one more rep.

And then the rule worth knowing before you install anything. One bad session never lowers the weight. Harder than planned, or a set that came up short — the weight stays, and the sentence says why:

Same weight. One set only got 4 reps, fewer than the 5 to 8 you aim for. One short session is no reason to go lighter, so try it again.

There is one more case that most progression schemes get wrong. If you started the lift easy and finished with nothing left — three reps of difference or more across the session — that is not a weight that is too heavy. It is you running out of gas between sets, and the fix is not less weight:

Same weight. You started with about 4 reps left in the tank and finished with nothing left in the tank. That is you getting tired during the session, not a weight that is too heavy. Try a longer rest between sets.

When it comes down

Rarely, and never because of one session.

On the default rule the weight does not drop at all. It drops on double progression, where a set under the bottom of your range is the signal, and it drops on a deload — which REGOL proposes and you accept or decline. Two sessions in a row harder than planned is what triggers the offer:

The last two sessions were harder than planned. Taking 70 kg usually costs one session; holding can cost several.

Accept it and about ten percent comes off for one session, after which the lift builds back. A deload week inside a block works the same way: half the sets, a little lighter, back to normal the week after.

When a rise is held back

Sometimes the session earns a rise and REGOL keeps the weight anyway, because the step would cost more than it gains. It always says which of these it is:

  • Three weeks or more since you last did it. “so get used to it again first. The step up comes next time.”
  • Fewer than half the planned sets logged. “which is too little to go on. It moves after a full session.”
  • The jump is too big for the range. If the next rung would drop you well below the reps you are aiming for, you add a rep first.
  • You are at the end of the equipment. The lightest setting, the heaviest plate — or, on an assisted machine already giving no help, “the next step is doing it unassisted”.

A lift you have never done

Nothing in the rules above works without a first number, so REGOL asks for one and then corrects it for you:

Pick a weight. You have not logged this one yet, so choose one you could lift for about 9 reps, with 2 left in the tank. The next set adjusts from your answer.

A set that lands far off becomes a test set — logged, but never used to drive a future weight — and the next set is aimed from what it told us. Four tries at most, then it settles: Close enough after 4 tries. Start from 45 kg, and REGOL adjusts it from here. Land it sooner and it says so: Found it. 45 kg is your working weight.

Other ways to progress

The rule above is the default. Per lift you can pick a different one, and the sentences change with it.

  • A set to failure. The last set runs out. Beat the number you got and the weight rises; otherwise “Beat that next time.”
  • Double progression. Reps carry the progress and the weight only moves at the edges of the range. “Add a rep before you add weight.”
  • Linear. Up every session, whatever happened. REGOL will not pretend it is reading anything: “This lift is set to go up every session, however the last one went.”
  • Imported history with no effort recorded. Most apps do not export how hard a set felt. REGOL goes by reps alone and tells you that is what it is doing: “You did not say how hard last time felt, so go by reps.”

During the workout

The decisions do not all wait until the end. Rest moves with the set you just logged — +45 s, that set came up short, or −30 s, that was easy — and a set you log changes the sets after it in the same lift:

An easy set steps the sets after it up, and a set that falls short of your reps steps them down.

Every number on the screen has a why? behind it, including that one.

Overruling it

Type any weight you like and REGOL takes it. It does not argue, and it does not quietly pretend the number was its own idea:

You set this weight yourself. REGOL proposed 80 kg for today.

The rep range is yours too, per lift, and it is the thing the whole rulebook is measured against. Set it where your training actually lives and the rest follows from it. If you want the general version of this question without the app in it, when to add weight is the shorter read.
