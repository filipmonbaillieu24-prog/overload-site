Four days a week

The upper/lower split

Two upper-body days, two lower-body days. Four sessions a week, everything trained twice, and a complete week here to take with you.

ON THIS PAGE The short answerThe weekWho it suitsOne thing to knowHow you progressTake it with you

The short answer

Upper/lower splits the week in half at the waist. Two sessions train everything above it, two train everything below. Over a week each muscle is trained twice, which is the main reason people choose it over a three-day split.

Twice a week is more total work than once, and that is the trade: four sessions instead of three, and you have to be able to show up for all four.

Who it suits

It suits you if four sessions a week is realistic and you have been lifting long enough that once-a-week frequency has stopped paying. It is also kind to a tight schedule: each session is shorter than a three-day split's, because the work is spread over more of them.

It suits you less if your week is unpredictable. Four fixed sessions is a lot to protect, and an upper/lower week that keeps losing days ends up as an accidental full-body programme without the design.

How you progress on it

A split decides what you train on which day. It does not decide whether you get stronger - that is what you do with the weight week to week.

The short version: finish the top of your rep range on every working set, or finish with more left in reserve than you planned, and the weight goes up. Neither, and it stays while you chase reps. When to add weight goes through both signals, and what RIR means covers the second one.
