The process

What happens to something you report.

  1. 1

    You file it from the report form, which writes the issue for you.

  2. 2

    I try to reproduce it. If I cannot, I ask you a question on the issue. You get an email, and answering it is usually what makes the fix possible.

  3. 3

    Once reproduced it is marked confirmed and given a severity: does it stop you training, is it annoying, or is it cosmetic. That is the order things get fixed in.

  4. 4

    If it is agreed but not soon it goes under Planned, with a comment saying what it is waiting for. That is a real answer, not a polite no.

  5. 5

    When it ships the issue closes, it appears under Shipped, and the change is in the next Play update.

Settled

Not on the list.

A few things are settled rather than open questions, so nobody spends a request on them. REGOL will not get an account system, ads, or a server that holds your training. And the engine will keep explaining itself: no suggestion will ever be a number with no reason attached.

iOS is different: it is wanted, and it sits under Planned rather than ruled out. It will not start until the Android app has stopped changing shape, because every decision made twice is a decision made badly once.

This page asks GitHub for the issue list from your browser. If GitHub is unreachable, or you block third-party requests, the lists stay empty and the links still work.
