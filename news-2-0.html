2.0
News/Version 2.0·10 November 2026

Two ways to run a workout, and nothing about your training changes.

The workout screen has been rebuilt and there are now two of it. Focus is the one you know, one set at a time, as large as the screen allows. List puts every set in a table and you tick them off. The same sets, the same weights and the same rest come out of both, and nothing you have already logged moves because of this update.

Focus or List

Pick the one that reads the way you train.

Focus is the screen you know: one set at a time, as large as the screen allows. List puts every set of every lift in a table — what you did last time, the weight, the reps and how many you had left — and you tick each one off, the way a paper log reads.

Choose under You › Make it yours › Live screen, and change your mind whenever you like, including in the middle of a workout: the sets you have logged stay logged.

Nothing about your training changes either way.
The Focus screen

Rebuilt, and it says what each set is.

The sets of a lift are a row of dots now, one for each, ticked as you log them; tap a logged one to correct it. Under the numbers, five words say what the set is — warm-up, normal, drop, rest-pause or AMRAP — and tapping one changes it on the spot.

Everything about one lift sits on a single sheet: warm-ups and sets, the rep range, how many reps to leave in reserve, the rest between sets and how the weight moves. Lifts can be dragged into a different order while you train.

“What’s the difference?” says which of the five move next time’s weight.
Reps

A chart for the lifts with nothing on them.

A lift done with no weight — chin-ups, reverse crunches — drew three flat charts, because all three measured the load. It is read in reps now: the working reps of each session added up, on its chart, its page, its row in Lifts and its share card.

Every other lift gets Reps as a fourth view beside estimated max, top set and volume.

Three flat charts, because all three measured the load.
Plan the hard sets

Drop sets and rest-pause, written into the template.

Under Set by set, a template can say which sets go to as many reps as possible, and add drop sets and rest-pause sets after the last one. They are set up in every workout from then on.

A drop set follows the weight you really lifted on the set before it, not the one that was planned.

Set up in every workout from then on.
Stretches

Eighty-six more, and not one asks for a weight.

The library has 86 more stretches, foam-roll releases and warm-up drills. Add a stretch to a workout and it is planned as a hold in seconds rather than as sets of reps, so nothing asks you for a weight.

That now applies to the stretches that were already in the library as well.

A hold in seconds, not three sets of eight.
Finding a lift

Type a name, and get that lift.

Type the name of a lift and the library used to answer with something else. “press” gave “Press Sit-Up”, “row” gave “Rowing, Stationary” — a cardio machine — and “curl” gave “curl-up”, which is an ab exercise.

A name that begins with the word you typed outranked one that merely contains it, and nothing in the library could settle it: “Barbell Curl” and “EZ-Bar Curl” carry the same equipment, level, mechanic and muscle. So 27 plain names now have one answer each, and every other search is untouched.

“row” gave a cardio machine.
Also in 2.0

Smaller things, put right.

The Milestone card opens as the full share studio like the other six. The full-screen rest and the top of the session summary take dark status icons where the accent runs edge to edge. Lift names get a third line where two cut them off. The year card and the all-time screen agree on how many hours you have been under the bar.

And the small ones: “1 minutes” on the watch, “02:08” in one rest bar against “2:08” in the other, and a picker titled “Add to Push” while you were editing Pull.

The set row on Focus follows the font size, and fades where it runs on.
From your feedback

What you asked for went in before it shipped.

Every reason REGOL gives for a weight is rewritten in plain words: first what it decided, then why. On the Focus screen a small “why?” beside the weight opens that reason. Each fix offered for a stalled lift says why it would work, and How REGOL works is rewritten in the same words.

A template can have a warm-up and a cool-down, logged but not counted. A lift that sits in several templates at different ranges can be brought into line from any of them. A lift added from the library now starts at a range that suits it, 6 to 10 for the big barbell lifts, 10 to 15 for curls and raises, planks in seconds; lifts you already have keep their numbers. And in List, a taken machine is no longer a dead end: jump to another lift and come back.

Same weight. You stopped with 2 reps left in the tank, as planned. Try for one more rep.
