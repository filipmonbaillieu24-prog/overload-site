How it works

The rules, in one place.

Nothing here is hidden in the app. Every rule has a sentence attached, and the sentence is what you see on the card.

Reps in reserve

How many more reps you could have done when you stopped. 0 is failure, 2 means two left. It is one tap per set. Skip it and the set is recorded at the effort you planned.

How the next weight is chosen

On the default rule the engine compares how much you had left with what you were aiming for, averaged over your sets. More in hand than planned earns weight, and so does clearing the top of your rep range on every set. Dead on target holds the weight and asks for one more rep. Under target holds rather than drops. Two sessions under in a row and a lighter week is offered. Two other rules exist per template or per lift: double progression (reps first, then weight) and linear (a step every session you complete).

How big the step is

From your own sets, not a fixed jump. Your best set says roughly what you could lift once; the engine walks up one step at a time and stops before a step that would leave you short of the bottom of your rep range. That is why one easy session on a 10 kg dumbbell never becomes 12.5 kg.

Set types

Normal sets drive progression. Warm-ups never count. Drop sets and rest-pause sets are logged but do not move the weight. A set to failure counts, and a big number there is the fastest way to a step up. Timed sets, like planks and carries, are logged in seconds and left out of proposals.

Templates, programs and blocks

A template is one workout. A program is an ordered rotation of templates; Today pins the next one. A block runs over weeks: the target reserve gets harder week by week, then an optional lighter week of half the sets. The week turns only once every template in it has been trained.

First time on a lift

No history means no proposal, so the first session finds your weight: aim for the middle of the rep range at the target reserve and log honestly. The next set is aimed from your answer.

Stalls

Four sessions over at least two weeks with no new estimated one-rep max and no new top weight count as a stall. The lift page offers a lighter session (about 90 percent), a wider rep range, or a swap.

A long break, or a short session

Three weeks away from a lift, or fewer than half the planned sets done, and the weight holds rather than steps up, with a sentence saying which it was.
