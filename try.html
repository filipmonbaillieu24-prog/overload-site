How it works

The rules the engine follows.

Everything the app does with your numbers, in one place.

Reps in reserve (RIR)+

How many more reps you could have done when you stopped. 0 is failure, 2 means two left. It is one tap per set and it is what makes the proposals good. Skip it and the set is recorded at the target you planned.

How the next weight is chosen+

With the RIR-gated rule: average your working sets’ RIR. One above target adds a step, two or more adds two. Clearing the top of the rep range on every set adds a step on its own. Under target holds the weight, and two sessions in a row under target propose a deload. Double progression adds reps first, then weight. Linear adds a step every session you complete.

Set types+

Normal sets drive progression. Warm-ups never count. Drop sets and rest-pause sets are logged but do not move the weight. AMRAP counts, and a big number there is the fastest way to a step up.

Templates, programs and blocks+

A template is one workout. A program is an ordered rotation of templates; Today pins the next one. A block is a mesocycle: the target RIR gets one harder every other week, then a deload week. The week only turns once every template in the block has been trained.

First time on a lift+

No history means no proposal, so the first session finds your weight: aim for the middle of the rep range at the target RIR and log honestly. Sets that miss become test sets, and the next one is aimed from your answer.

Stalls and deloads+

No new estimated 1RM or top weight across four sessions over at least two weeks counts as a stall. The lift page and the Lifts list offer a deload next time (about 90%), a wider rep range, or a swap.

Your data+

No account and no server. One JSON file on the phone, exported or backed up whenever you like, importable from Strong and Hevy CSVs. The web version here keeps nothing at all: reload the page and it starts fresh.
